# Problem Statement & Opportunity Assessment

## Document Purpose

This document separates the product idea from the underlying customer problem.
It frames the demand-side logic for why FOS might deserve to exist and what must
be validated before deeper investment.

## Core Problem Statement

Families often lack a shared, low-friction way to make everyday contribution
visible, recognized, and negotiable. As a result, effort goes unseen, fairness
is argued emotionally instead of discussed clearly, and children do not build a
strong connection between contribution and reward.

## Primary Customer Pain Points

### Parents Or Partners Carrying More Household Load

- feel their effort is invisible or treated as normal;
- accumulate resentment over time;
- dislike repeatedly asking others to help;
- struggle to prove imbalance without sounding confrontational.

### Parents Or Partners Carrying Less Or Less Visible Load

- may not realize the scale of unseen work;
- can interpret complaints as criticism instead of evidence;
- may want to contribute more but lack prompts, structure, or motivation.

### Children In The Household

- often do not understand how contribution connects to family well-being;
- respond better to concrete feedback than abstract moral lessons;
- lose motivation when rewards feel inconsistent or unclear.

## Emotional And Operational Consequences

When this problem stays unresolved, families experience both emotional and
operational costs:

- repeated micro-conflicts about who does more;
- tension around asking for help;
- unequal mental load on one parent;
- inconsistent discipline and reward patterns for children;
- lower appreciation between family members;
- tasks still getting done, but with hidden frustration.

## Why Existing Alternatives Fall Short

Families already try to solve this through:

- memory and verbal discussion;
- chat messages;
- task apps and shared to-do lists;
- allowance charts or reward boards;
- spreadsheet-like tracking.

These substitutes have structural gaps:

- they track chores but not recognition;
- they support assignment but not relational fairness;
- they support rewards but not family-wide contribution context;
- they often feel administrative, not emotionally acceptable;
- they are rarely designed for mixed users such as parents and children.

## Opportunity Gap

The market gap is not just a missing task manager. The gap is a missing family
coordination layer that balances:

- visibility without harshness;
- incentives without commodifying relationships;
- parent-child motivation without over-parenting;
- fairness without turning family life into a scoreboard.

This creates an opportunity for a product positioned between:

- family organizer apps;
- allowance/reward apps;
- habit or chore trackers;
- lightweight social systems.

## Why Vietnam Is A Reasonable First-Market Assumption

Vietnam is a plausible starting point because:

- family involvement remains high across generations;
- household roles can still be unequally distributed in practice;
- indirect communication norms can make explicit requests difficult;
- parents are increasingly comfortable with mobile-first family utilities;
- there is room for a local-language product with culturally legible framing.

## Value Hypotheses To Validate

The project should treat these as hypotheses, not facts:

### H1. Visibility Hypothesis

If contribution becomes visible in a shared timeline, resentment decreases and
discussion becomes easier.

### H2. Recognition Hypothesis

If family members can recognize each other explicitly, emotional satisfaction
increases even when workload does not change immediately.

### H3. Motivation Hypothesis

If children receive clear points and rewards for contribution, participation
frequency increases.

### H4. Respectful Request Hypothesis

If rewards and requests happen through a structured but voluntary flow, users
feel safer asking for what they want.

### H5. Fairness Insight Hypothesis

If households can see contribution patterns over time, they will self-correct
imbalance without requiring strict enforcement.

## Barriers To Adoption

The biggest adoption risks are:

- feeling judged or monitored;
- believing family love should not be measured;
- finding the setup process too heavy;
- disagreement between adults about fairness rules;
- children gaming the system;
- low habit retention after novelty fades.

## Opportunity Assessment Summary

The product opportunity is strongest if FOS can demonstrate that it is:

- emotionally safer than a chore tracker;
- more actionable than a family chat thread;
- more holistic than a reward board;
- simpler than a household management platform.

If the product cannot maintain this balance, it risks becoming either:

- too soft to change behavior, or
- too transactional to feel appropriate for family life.
