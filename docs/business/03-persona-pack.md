# Persona Pack

## Document Purpose

This document defines the minimum persona set for MVP planning. These personas
are not demographic stereotypes; they are behavior patterns the product must
support well in order to create adoption and trust.

## Persona 1: The Primary Load Carrier

### Short Profile

A parent who carries a large share of visible and invisible household work.
Often the planner, reminder-holder, or default fallback person.

### Typical Context

- Age range often 28-42
- May be working full time
- Uses messaging apps constantly
- Feels family logistics live in their head

### Goals

- Make effort visible without starting another argument
- Feel appreciated more consistently
- Rebalance contribution without repeatedly nagging
- Teach children to contribute more responsibly

### Motivations

- Reduce resentment
- Create a more cooperative home environment
- Build better family habits
- Gain emotional recognition, not only practical help

### Frustrations

- "No one notices what I do unless I stop doing it"
- "If I ask, I sound controlling"
- "I have to manage both the work and the reminders"
- "Rewarding children feels inconsistent"

### Technology Habits

- Comfortable with mobile apps
- Likely to adopt the tool first
- Will tolerate some setup if clear value is immediate

### Sensitivity To Being Scored

Medium. This user usually wants fairness visibility, but may reject a system
that also makes them feel cold or overly administrative.

### What Would Make Them Churn

- Data entry feels like extra unpaid labor
- Other family members do not participate
- The app shows numbers but does not improve family dynamics
- Emotional care is flattened into generic points

## Persona 2: The Less-Structured Contributor

### Short Profile

A parent or partner who contributes in real ways, but less consistently or less
visibly. They may feel criticized by tracking systems or may genuinely want more
clarity on how to help.

### Typical Context

- Age range often 28-45
- Busy with work or mentally less tuned to domestic routines
- Uses apps selectively based on convenience

### Goals

- Understand what matters without being micromanaged
- Contribute more clearly and get credit for it
- Avoid conflict or defensiveness
- Engage in family routines with lower friction

### Motivations

- Reduce household tension
- Feel competent instead of blamed
- Participate in a system that feels fair and lightweight

### Frustrations

- "I do things too, but they are not counted"
- "I do not want to be audited at home"
- "If the app feels childish or controlling, I will stop using it"

### Technology Habits

- Adopts only if onboarding is simple
- Prefers low-maintenance interactions
- More likely to respond to reminders and clear flows than to explore deeply

### Sensitivity To Being Scored

High. This persona is vulnerable to shame or resistance if the product feels
judgmental.

### What Would Make Them Churn

- Public comparison that feels embarrassing
- Too many rules or categories
- No flexibility to explain context
- Reward/request flows that feel manipulative

## Persona 3: The Child Contributor

### Short Profile

A child in the family old enough to understand simple tasks, rewards, and basic
feedback loops. Parents mediate most rules.

### Typical Context

- Broad age range, roughly school-age
- Motivated by visible progress and clear rewards
- Understands app interactions more easily when concrete and playful

### Goals

- Know what to do
- Earn rewards fairly
- Feel proud and recognized
- See progress in a fun way

### Motivations

- Tangible rewards
- Praise and recognition
- Sense of achievement
- Friendly routine and playful feedback

### Frustrations

- Rules that change unpredictably
- Adults disagreeing about whether they earned something
- Rewards feeling impossible to reach
- Contribution not being acknowledged

### Technology Habits

- Comfortable with visual and game-like interactions
- Short attention span for text-heavy flows
- Relies on parents for setup and trust

### Sensitivity To Being Scored

Mixed. Children may enjoy progress systems, but negative scoring or harsh
comparisons can reduce confidence quickly.

### What Would Make Them Churn

- Tasks feel like punishment
- Rewards seem unfair or unattainable
- The app is too text-heavy or parent-centric
- Adults use it only to track failure

## Shared Design Implications

Across these personas, the product must:

- minimize data-entry burden for the primary load carrier;
- avoid shame-heavy comparison for the less-structured contributor;
- keep reward logic explainable for children;
- allow recognition and narrative, not only numbers;
- make the system feel supportive rather than disciplinary.

## Persona Hierarchy For MVP

Priority order for MVP decision-making:

1. Primary load carrier
2. Less-structured contributor
3. Child contributor

This order matters because adoption is likely to start with the primary load
carrier, but retention depends on not alienating the second adult and on making
the child experience coherent.
