# Risk, Assumptions & Validation Plan

## Document Purpose

This document captures the major assumptions behind FOS, the risks that could
break the concept, and the validation work needed before the team commits to
deeper product investment.

## Core Product Assumptions

- Families want a shared tool for contribution visibility.
- The problem is painful enough that a dedicated product is justified.
- A fairness-plus-recognition framing is more acceptable than strict task
  tracking.
- Households are willing to sustain a simple routine if the value appears fast.

## MVP Focus Assumption

MVP assumes the clearest entry wedge is the parent-child contribution/reward
loop, supported by parent-managed child profiles and simple default household
rules.

Adult fairness visibility is still valuable, but is treated as secondary in MVP
when tradeoffs appear.

## Behavioral Assumptions

- Adults will log contributions if the interaction is lightweight.
- At least one adult will champion household adoption.
- The second adult will participate if the system does not feel accusatory.
- Children respond positively to consistent point/reward loops.
- Recognition signals can improve emotional satisfaction, not just task
  completion.

## Monetization Assumptions

Monetization is intentionally unresolved. Current assumptions to test later:

- families may pay for premium coaching, insights, or richer family tools;
- freemium may be more realistic than paid-only entry;
- monetization should not distort the emotional tone of the product.

## Market And Cultural Risks In Vietnam

- Some users may reject the idea of quantifying family care.
- Traditional family roles may make fairness transparency socially sensitive.
- Parents may embrace child reward logic but reject partner-to-partner reward
  exchange.
- Different regions and family norms may respond differently to visible scoring.

## Ethical Risks

- The product may over-commodify emotional labor.
- Reward mechanics may create manipulation rather than appreciation.
- Comparison views may shame lower-participation members.
- Parents may use the product as a punishment ledger rather than a supportive
  ritual.

## Adoption Risks

- Setup burden falls on the person already carrying too much load.
- The second adult refuses participation.
- Children find the rules inconsistent.
- The novelty wears off before habit value is established.

## Product Design Risks

- The point model is too simplistic to feel fair.
- The point model is too complex to explain.
- Love Points feel artificial.
- Rewards feel too transactional.
- Timeline visibility feels like monitoring.

## Validation Plan

### Phase 1: Problem Validation Interviews

Target:

- parents carrying high household load;
- parents in dual-income homes;
- families with school-age children.

Questions to validate:

- How is household contribution discussed today?
- Where does unfairness feel strongest?
- What makes direct requests uncomfortable?
- What kinds of recognition feel meaningful versus awkward?
- Would a shared app feel helpful or intrusive?

### Phase 2: Concept Validation

Test materials:

- concept narrative;
- core flow mockups;
- sample contribution and reward scenarios.

Questions to validate:

- Does the concept feel useful?
- Does the concept feel emotionally acceptable?
- Which parts feel motivating, childish, or transactional?
- Which family members would likely adopt or resist?

### Phase 3: Prototype/Beta Validation

Observe:

- onboarding completion;
- contribution logging frequency;
- reward/request usage;
- adult participation balance;
- reported fairness impact.

## Decision Gates

Continue investing if:

- interviewees strongly recognize the problem;
- the concept feels more helpful than uncomfortable;
- multiple-member household participation occurs in early testing;
- repeat contribution behavior appears beyond first-use novelty.

Reframe the product if:

- users consistently reject the idea of scoring or recognition tracking;
- partner dynamics feel too sensitive for the proposed framing;
- families prefer pure task organization over fairness/reward mechanics.

Reduce scope if:

- parent-child value is clear but partner-to-partner value is weak;
- recognition features work but reward mechanics feel too transactional.

## Open Validation Questions

- Is the strongest wedge parent-child behavior or adult fairness visibility?
- Which rewards feel healthy versus inappropriate?
- What is the acceptable level of approval friction?
- How should emotional-care recognition appear without feeling artificial?
- What language best frames the system in Vietnamese without sounding corporate
  or cold?

The first question should now be interpreted as a validation check on the chosen
MVP wedge, not as an unresolved product direction.
